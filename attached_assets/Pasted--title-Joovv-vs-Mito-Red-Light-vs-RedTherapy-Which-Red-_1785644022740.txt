# Joovv vs Mito Red Light vs RedTherapy: Which One Actually Fits You

I get some version of this question almost weekly now: "Which red light do I buy?" And for a while my answer was just "Joovv, it's the gold standard" — which is true, but it's also the answer that ignores that most people asking me this aren't trying to build a home clinic. They're trying to fix one specific thing: their skin, their joints, their sleep, their recovery.

So instead of one answer, here's the honest breakdown of the three I actually recommend, based on what each one is genuinely built for.

## The short version

| | Joovv | Mito Red Light | RedTherapy (RedRush) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Built for** | Full-body, modular, clinic-grade | Targeted — face, neck, skin | Full-body, budget-conscious power |
| **Wavelengths** | 660nm + 850nm | Varies by device, 2–6 wavelengths depending on model | 660nm + 850nm, pulsed |
| **Entry price** | $549 (handheld Go 2.0) | $329 (MitoCLEAR mask) | $675 (RedRush 400 Pulse) |
| **Full-body price** | $1,699–$11,099 | Panels from $1,299 | $675–$2,490 |
| **Certification** | FDA-cleared general wellness, independently certified to medical electrical safety standards | FDA Class II cleared, IEC 60601 | General wellness device, not FDA-cleared |
| **Best for** | People who want the most clinically documented option and don't mind paying for it | People whose main goal is skin — acne, fine lines, tone | People who want real full-body power without the flagship price tag |

## Joovv — the benchmark, if budget isn't the constraint

Joovv is still the name that shows up in the most published photobiomodulation research, and that matters if you actually care about the evidence behind what you're buying rather than just the light itself. The modular system is the other thing that sold me — you can start with a single panel and physically expand it later instead of replacing the whole unit when your needs grow.

The catch is obvious: price. A real full-body setup runs well into four figures, and you're paying a premium for the brand and the certification, not necessarily for irradiance you can't get elsewhere for less. If you want the most-studied option and the price doesn't scare you off, this is still where I'd point you.

## Mito Red Light — the one I recommend for skin specifically

This is the category Joovv doesn't really compete in. Mito's mask line (MitoGLOW, MitoCLEAR) is FDA Class II cleared and built specifically around facial coverage — acne, fine lines, tone, texture — at a fraction of full-panel pricing. If your actual goal is "I want my skin to look better," a $329–$499 mask that's purpose-built for the face is a smarter buy than a $1,700 full-body panel you'll mostly use on your face anyway.

They also make full panels (MitoPRO line) if you want to go bigger later, so it's not a dead end if your goals expand.

## RedTherapy — the value play for full-body power

This is the one I recommend most to people who want real full-body red light without committing to Joovv money. The RedRush panels run the same core wavelength combination (660nm/850nm) in a pulsed format, and you're getting legitimate full-body coverage starting under $700 — less than a third of Joovv's full-body entry point.

What you're giving up is the clinical pedigree and the independent certification Joovv has invested in. That doesn't mean it doesn't work — it means you're trusting the manufacturer's numbers more than a third party's. For most people using this for general recovery and circulation rather than treating a diagnosed condition, that trade-off is reasonable.

## So which one do you actually buy

- **Recovery, inflammation, general full-body use, and price is genuinely not a factor:** Joovv.
- **Your main goal is your face — acne, aging, tone:** Mito Red Light's mask line. Don't overspend on a full panel for a facial goal.
- **You want real full-body coverage and power without flagship pricing:** RedTherapy.

None of these are wrong choices. The wrong choice is buying the most expensive one because it's the most famous name when what you actually need is a $329 mask, or buying a mask when what you actually need is full-body coverage for joint recovery.
