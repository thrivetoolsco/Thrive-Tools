## How does SYB shielding work?

The design choice that defines SYB is that the shielding sits on one side only, the side facing your body. The company says this is deliberate. A phone in the pouch can still receive calls and messages and its battery drains normally, because the signal is not blocked on the side facing away from you. The shielding material is a fine metallic mesh woven into the fabric. SYB says its products are laboratory tested up to 20 GHz, which covers 5G frequencies, and that they deflect up to 99 percent of wireless EMF radiation. The canopy is tested up to 26 GHz. Those are the company's test claims, and they describe shielding performance, not a health result. SYB itself says the only way to know if shielding is working is to test it with an EMF meter, because a phone that keeps its signal inside a pouch may simply be boosting its power to compensate for interference.

## Does EMF shielding actually work?

This question splits into two, and the answers are different.

First, does the material block radiofrequency fields? Shielding fabric is a real, measurable thing. A conductive mesh attenuates radiofrequency fields, and that attenuation can be measured with a meter. SYB publishes lab test results for its products, and independent reviewers who have measured SYB products with meters have reported large reductions. So the narrow claim, that the material reduces the field on the shielded side, is testable and plausible.

Second, does using a shield improve your health? No human study has shown that. That is the honest gap in the whole category, and it matters more than the first question.

## What does the science actually say about EMF and health?

Here is where the evidence stands, as of late 2026, with both sides of it.

The World Health Organization commissioned a set of 12 systematic reviews on radiofrequency EMF and health. Their findings, summarised by the Swiss expert group BERENIS in December 2025, were that the human observational studies on cancer, cognition, reproduction and symptoms did not identify evidence of adverse health effects from RF-EMF exposure. A 2024 WHO-commissioned review of human studies (Karipidis and colleagues, Environment International) found no association between mobile phone use and brain cancer. Australia's radiation protection agency, ARPANSA, reviewed the same evidence and concluded there is no substantiated evidence that low-level RF-EMF exposure poses health risks in human populations. The International Agency for Research on Cancer classified radiofrequency fields as Group 2B, "possibly carcinogenic," in 2011. That classification means limited evidence, not established harm.

The other side of the picture comes mostly from animal studies. The US National Toxicology Program (2018) and the Ramazzini Institute (2018) both reported increased rates of heart schwannomas and brain gliomas in rats exposed to whole-body radiofrequency fields at doses far above everyday human exposure. A 2025 WHO-commissioned animal review (Mevissen and colleagues) rated the evidence for those two tumours in animals as high certainty, again at high doses. A separate 2025 review (La Rocca and colleagues) found moderate-to-high certainty evidence that mobile device exposure impairs sperm quality in human and animal studies.

Put together, the fair summary is this. At the exposure levels of everyday device use, human studies have not established a health risk. At much higher doses in animals, effects have been found. Nothing in that picture proves that a shielding pouch changes a health outcome, and nothing in it proves the opposite either. If you buy SYB, you are buying a precaution, and you should know that is what you are buying.

## How to reduce EMF exposure for free

Before spending anything, do the things that cost nothing and do the most.

- Keep distance. Exposure falls fast as distance grows. Do not carry your phone in your pocket when you can put it in a bag, and do not work with a laptop directly on your lap.
- Use airplane mode at night, and keep the phone out of the bedroom or across the room while you sleep.
- Use speakerphone or wired headphones for long calls instead of holding the phone to your head.
- Turn off WiFi and Bluetooth when you are not using them, and put the router on a timer or unplug it overnight.
- Use a desk for the laptop instead of your lap.

These five habits reduce your exposure more than any single product, and they are free. A pouch is an addition to them, not a replacement.
