## What's in IM8 Daily Ultimate Essentials PRO?

This is where most reviews go shallow. I'm going to go through the key systems the formula targets and what's actually there.

### Vitamins and Minerals — The Foundation

The PRO formula uses P5P (pyridoxal-5-phosphate) for B6, methylcobalamin for B12, and Quatrefolic for folate — all bioactive forms at clinical doses. Vitamin D3 is dosed at 1,200 IU paired with 40mcg of K2.

Why does this matter? A large portion of the population has reduced ability to convert synthetic vitamin forms into usable ones. Folic acid requires conversion to methylfolate — people with MTHFR gene variants (roughly 40-60% of the population) do this poorly or not at all. Cyanocobalamin B12 requires conversion to methylcobalamin. If your supplement uses the synthetic versions, you may be absorbing far less than the label suggests.

The most notable upgrade in the PRO version is B12, which increased from 24mcg to 200mcg and switched to methylcobalamin — a 733% increase in potency. That's not a cosmetic update. That's a meaningful nutritional improvement.

### Gut Health — A Four-Layer System

IM8 uses a 4-layer gut health system: 3g prebiotics, 10 billion CFU from 2 spore-forming strains, FloraSMART postbiotics at 25mg, plus digestive enzymes.

The spore-based probiotic strains (Bacillus subtilis DE111 and Bacillus coagulans BC99) are what separate IM8's gut protocol from most competitors. Standard Lactobacillus and Bifidobacterium strains are fragile — they're largely destroyed by stomach acid before reaching the colon. Spore-forming bacteria produce a protective endospore that survives digestion and colonizes the gut. This isn't marketing language — it's a genuine mechanistic difference backed by research.

The inclusion of postbiotics (beneficial metabolites produced by bacteria) and digestive enzymes alongside prebiotics and probiotics makes this a complete gut protocol, not just a probiotic add-on.

### Cardiovascular and Mitochondrial Health

CoQ10 is dosed at 100mg — a clinically meaningful amount for cardiovascular support and cellular energy production.

Most all-in-one supplements either skip CoQ10 entirely or include token amounts of 10-20mg that don't reflect therapeutic dosing. 100mg is what appears in the research on CoQ10 for energy metabolism and heart health. This is one of the standout inclusions in the formula for biohackers specifically.

### Joint and Muscle Support

MSM (methylsulfonylmethane) is dosed at 1,000mg — enough to support joint health, connective tissue integrity, and post-exercise recovery. Again, most multis skip this entirely.

### Cognitive Support — The PRO Addition

The genuinely new addition in the PRO formula is 30mg of Saffron Extract, added specifically to support cognitive function and mood balance. That's an area the original formula didn't directly target.

Saffron at 30mg has a growing body of human research behind it — particularly for mood regulation, with several trials showing effects comparable to low-dose SSRIs in mild-to-moderate depression. It's an unconventional but genuinely interesting addition for a daily supplement.

## The Honest Criticisms

No review I write is worth reading if I don't tell you what's not perfect. Here's what the legitimate criticisms are.

**Some ingredients are in supporting rather than therapeutic doses.** One independent review noted that the adaptogen and digestive enzyme inclusions may be below fully therapeutic levels. The digestive enzymes are likely not abundant enough to enhance gut health in fully noticeable ways on their own. The core vitamins, minerals, CoQ10, MSM, and probiotic doses are solid — but some of the secondary inclusions are more "supporting cast" than starring roles.

**The celebrity co-founder angle is real marketing.** David Beckham is a co-founder. His name and face are front and center. Some people find this a reason for skepticism — and it's fair to wonder how much of the premium goes toward brand-building versus formulation quality. My read: the scientific advisory board's involvement is genuine and the formula reflects it, but the marketing machine is also real.

**It's not cheap.** At roughly $2.61 per serving on the quarterly plan, it's a premium product. The cost-per-supplement-replaced math works in its favour if you're already running a comprehensive stack — but if you're new to supplementation and just want a multivitamin, you can find solid options for less.

**Not NSF Certified for Sport specifically.** IM8 is NSF Certified but doesn't currently hold the NSF Certified for Sport designation, which is specifically relevant for competitive athletes subject to drug testing. For the general population this doesn't matter — but it's worth knowing if you're a competitive athlete.
