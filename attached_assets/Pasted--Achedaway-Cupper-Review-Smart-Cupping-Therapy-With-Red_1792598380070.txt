## How does smart cupping work?

Traditional cupping creates negative pressure inside a cup placed on the skin, drawing the skin and superficial tissue upward. The idea behind the practice is to increase local blood flow to the area and support the body's normal recovery process after training or physical strain.

"Smart" cupping changes two things:

1. **Dynamic suction.** Rather than one continuous pull, the Cupper alternates between suction and release in a rhythm. Achedaway says this rhythmic alternation is designed to boost blood circulation to the cupping area faster than a static cup, and to make sessions more comfortable and controllable.
2. **Built-in red light.** The device emits red light in the 630–670 nm wavelength range while it works — the same general red-light range used by many home red light therapy devices — which Achedaway positions as an aid for relaxation and for supporting skin and muscle tissue during recovery.

Because the suction is motor-driven and adjustable (the specified suction intensity range is −60 to 0 kPa), there's no flame, no alcohol-soaked cotton ball, and no manual hand pump — the parts of traditional cupping that make it impractical to do on yourself.

## How to use the Achedaway Cupper

A typical session is straightforward:

1. Choose the cup attachment size that suits the area — larger cups for the back, quads, and glutes; smaller ones for tighter areas.
2. Place the Cupper on clean, dry skin and power it on so it seals and begins suction.
3. Set your intensity and session length, either on the device or in the Achedaway App. If you're new to cupping, start at a low intensity and a short session and build up gradually.
