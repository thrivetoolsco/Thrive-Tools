## What Should You Take First Thing in the Morning?

The first thing to take in the morning is water — not supplements and not coffee. After 7-9 hours of fasting, mild dehydration is universal. Mild dehydration activates a low-grade stress response that elevates cortisol before the day has started. 500ml of water with a pinch of sea salt before anything else reverses overnight fluid deficit and primes the system for everything that follows.

**The correct morning sequence:**
1. Water with electrolytes (first 5 minutes)
2. Morning light outside — no sunglasses, 5-10 minutes
3. Foundation supplements with water or food
4. Tonic herb drink or ceremonial cacao
5. Coffee — 90-120 minutes after waking, not immediately

## Why Should You Delay Coffee 90 Minutes After Waking?

Delaying coffee 90-120 minutes after waking produces longer, more stable energy and eliminates the 2pm crash. The mechanism: caffeine blocks adenosine receptors but doesn't eliminate adenosine. Adenosine continues clearing from the brain for the first 90 minutes after waking. Coffee taken immediately blocks receptors before clearance completes — when caffeine wears off, uncleared adenosine floods back, causing the afternoon crash.

Waiting 90-120 minutes allows adenosine to clear naturally. Caffeine then blocks active receptors rather than ones still clearing, producing longer-lasting energy without the rebound crash.

A secondary reason: cortisol peaks naturally in the first 60-90 minutes of waking. Adding caffeine on top of peak cortisol amplifies the cortisol spike unnecessarily and disrupts the HPA axis over time.

## What Supplements Should NOT Be Taken in the Morning?

**Melatonin** — melatonin is a darkness hormone. Taking it in the morning suppresses the cortisol awakening response, disrupts the circadian rhythm, and causes daytime drowsiness. Reserve for jet lag only at low doses (0.5mg).

**High-dose magnesium glycinate** — the glycine component has calming effects that work against morning alertness for some people. Better timed in the evening.

**Sedating adaptogens** — valerian, passionflower, and high-dose ashwagandha in some people produce drowsiness. Better taken in the evening.

**Iron supplements** — best taken on an empty stomach away from other supplements, particularly calcium and magnesium, which impair absorption. If prescribed iron, take separately from your morning stack.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can you take all supplements at once in the morning?**
Yes — most foundational supplements have no significant interactions and can be taken together. Exceptions: iron should be taken separately from calcium and magnesium. Fat-soluble supplements (D3, K2, omega-3, urolithin A) require a meal with fat for optimal absorption. Everything else in the morning stack can be combined without concern.

**Should supplements be taken with food or without?**
Fat-soluble supplements always with food. Water-soluble vitamins and most minerals are fine with or without food. Spore-based probiotics work regardless of food timing — the endospore survives either way. Creatine is flexible — food or fasted, timing doesn't meaningfully affect outcomes.

**How long before morning supplements produce noticeable effects?**
Energy from bioactive B vitamins: days to weeks. Cognitive effects from Lion's Mane: 2-4 weeks. Adaptogenic effects from tonic herbs: 4-6 weeks of consistent use. Gut health improvements from spore-based probiotics: 2-6 weeks. Longevity compound benefits (urolithin A, NMN, spermidine): months of consistent use. Creatine saturation: 3-4 weeks at 5g daily.

**What is the minimum morning supplement routine for maximum impact?**
If you can only do three things: magnesium (glycinate, not oxide), vitamin D3 with K2, and omega-3 in triglyceride form. These three address the highest-prevalence deficiencies with the widest downstream benefits. Add IM8 PRO when ready to consolidate everything into one scoop.

**Should you take supplements on an empty stomach in the morning?**
Most supplements are fine on an empty stomach. The exception: fat-soluble supplements (vitamin D, K2, omega-3, urolithin A, CoQ10) require dietary fat for absorption. Take these with your first meal of the day. Everything else — B vitamins, magnesium, adaptogens, probiotics, creatine — can be taken fasted or fed without meaningful difference in outcome.

**Is it okay to mix supplements into coffee?**
Heat above 60°C can degrade some heat-sensitive compounds. Do not mix probiotics, enzymes, or vitamin C into hot coffee. Creatine, magnesium, and most adaptogens are heat-stable and fine in coffee. Better practice: take supplements in water first, then have coffee separately 90-120 minutes after waking.
